# Beginner pathway

**Goal:** your first 10 solves, in an order that builds confidence. **Who:** new players with no CTF experience.

## Why this order
Player data shows **analytical** challenges (Forensics, OSINT, Crypto, Steganography) have a 46.7% completion rate, against 28.0% for **technical** ones (Web, Reverse Engineering, Binary, Programming). So start analytical, then move to technical. [See the data](../insights/metrics.md).

## Stage 1 — Get comfortable (solves 1–4)
| Category | Why |
|---|---|
| Warm up | Learn how flags and submission work |
| OSINT | Highest completion rate (83%): research skills, no tools needed |
| Forensics | Look inside files and find hidden data |

## Stage 2 — Build patterns (solves 5–7)
| Category | Why |
|---|---|
| Linux | Practise the webshell |
| Cryptography | Recognise common encodings and ciphers |
| Steganography | Find data hidden in images and audio |

## Stage 3 — First technical steps (solves 8–10)
| Category | Why |
|---|---|
| Web | Inspect and change how websites behave |
| Binary | Your first look at how programs run |

## How to practise
- Steady, regular sessions beat long bursts. The most effective players made 101–150 attempts across the platform, not the most attempts.
- After each solve, read one other player's write-up.
- Pick a direction after Stage 3: go deep in one category (specialist) or broad across many (generalist). Both work.
